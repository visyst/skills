# Output Templates

Choose the requested depth. Headings may change to fit the company, but preserve the underlying coverage.

## Evidence labels

Use these distinctions without cluttering every paragraph:

- **Direct founder choice:** Explicitly selected, stated, or confirmed by the founder.
- **Strategic synthesis:** A conclusion drawn from several answers.
- **Intended advantage:** A differentiation or USP the company aims to prove.
- **Intentionally open:** A decision deliberately deferred until named evidence or experience exists.
- **Flexible:** A choice that does not need one permanent parent-brand answer.
- **Unanswered gap:** Missing information that still prevents a responsible conclusion.

Do not convert an aspiration into current fact.

Maintain these labels in working notes for every major claim. The final narrative need not carry inline labels, but its closing evidence notes must map each mission, audience, principle, positioning claim, and intended advantage to its status and interview basis.

## Comprehensive brand foundation

Write for a designer, writer, collaborator, or future team member. Prefer a coherent narrative over a stack of disconnected answers.

The section list is a coverage map, not a completion mandate. Merge or omit any section unsupported by the interview. Mark a material omission as unanswered rather than inventing content to make the document look complete.

### Opening metadata

- legal and public name, when relevant;
- location or origin, when relevant;
- current company stage;
- purpose of the document.

### 1. Executive portrait

Describe:

- what the company is today;
- what it is becoming;
- the central customer or problem;
- what it should become known for;
- the commercial or organizational transition underway.

### 2. Brand essentials

Make conventional answers easy to locate:

- mission;
- 5–10 year vision;
- core values expressed as behavior;
- primary, secondary, and current audiences;
- personality and voice;
- desired emotions and associations;
- tagline or brand-line status;
- differentiation;
- intended USP and proof status;
- key message hierarchy.

If the founder never used these corporate terms, write the answers from evidence without pretending they dictated the wording.

### 3. Origin and reason to exist

Cover:

- founder context;
- history and present economic reality;
- dissatisfaction or opportunity that created the company;
- what the company moves away from and toward;
- capabilities worth carrying forward.

### 4. Ambition and business shape

Cover:

- desired scale;
- ownership and revenue model;
- product/service relationship;
- team or portfolio shape;
- private measures of success;
- near-term transition versus long-term destination.

### 5. Audience and problem

Distinguish:

- primary future audience;
- secondary opportunity audiences;
- current commercial audience;
- excluded or poor-fit audiences;
- audience situation and motivation;
- demographic questions that must remain product-specific;
- the problems worth sustained attention.

### 6. Product, service, or opportunity thesis

Document:

- promising territories;
- selection filters;
- economic and emotional criteria;
- what the company refuses to create;
- boundaries around technology, data, quality, and ethics.

### 7. Positioning and intended advantage

Include:

- internal positioning statement;
- customer alternatives, including doing nothing;
- competitive frame to seek;
- frames to avoid;
- intended USP;
- evidence already available;
- evidence still required.

Avoid declaring uniqueness merely because the wording sounds distinctive.

### 8. Character and productive tensions

Define:

- central personality combination;
- supporting qualities;
- anti-identity;
- productive tensions using “X without becoming Y”;
- behavior that makes each quality observable.

### 9. Experience and trust

Describe:

- desired emotional aftertaste;
- the order in which feelings should develop;
- trust signals;
- product or service experience principles;
- support posture;
- response to mistakes;
- privacy and human-control expectations.

When evidence supports it, map discovery, evaluation, commitment, first value, continued use, support, renewal, recommendation, and departure.

### 10. Principles expressed as behavior

Use 4–7 principles. For each:

- use a memorable plain-language title;
- explain the action it requires;
- show the trade-off it resolves;
- avoid abstract virtue words without behavioral meaning.

### 11. Voice and language

Include:

- defining voice quality;
- supporting qualities and limits;
- writing principles;
- language to favor;
- language to avoid;
- behavior in ordinary, celebratory, and difficult interactions.

### 12. Brand architecture and founder relationship

When relevant, specify:

- parent versus product or service brands;
- endorsement model;
- founder visibility;
- role of the legal name;
- naming elements that need or do not need explanation.

### 13. Culture, place, and social posture

Cover only what evidence supports:

- geographic and cultural influence;
- founder identity and risk of false assumptions;
- ethical commitments expressed through action;
- political or social boundaries;
- stereotypes or borrowed causes to avoid.

Do not invent a public political position.

### 14. Creative direction

Provide strategy, not visual design:

- desired atmosphere;
- what the metaphor means and does not mean;
- emotional and sensory balances;
- current identity inventory;
- fixed assets;
- undefined visual-system components;
- signals the identity must avoid;
- evaluation criteria for a designer.

### 15. Messaging and engagement

Include:

- ordered key messages;
- short and expanded company descriptions;
- channel roles;
- online engagement behavior;
- offline engagement behavior;
- present-versus-future messaging;
- topics that should remain quiet or contextual.

### 16. Guardrails

Provide:

- “[Company] is / is not” contrasts;
- strategic-fit questions;
- emotional-fit questions;
- practical-fit questions;
- language-fit questions.

### 17. What remains open

Explain:

- deliberately deferred decisions and why certainty would be premature;
- external hypotheses awaiting evidence;
- unanswered gaps that still require founder input;
- flexible choices that do not need one parent-brand answer;
- what evidence or milestone should resolve each deferred choice.

### 18. Decision summary

End with 8–15 concise decisions a collaborator can use when uncertain.

### 19. Source and interpretation notes

Separate:

- direct founder choices;
- strategic synthesis;
- intended advantages awaiting proof;
- intentionally open, flexible, and unanswered items;
- an evidence map linking every major conclusion to a specific answer, trade-off, or observed fact;
- recommended revisit point.

## Quality review

Before delivery, test every major statement:

1. Which interview evidence supports this?
2. Could this sentence describe many unrelated companies?
3. Is aspiration being mistaken for present reality?
4. Does a collaborator know what decision this should influence?
5. Is a productive tension being flattened into a vague adjective?
6. Has the founder's natural meaning survived the strategic wording?
7. Does each principle contain an observable behavior, trade-off, concrete situation, or prohibited alternative?
8. Have unsupported sections been omitted or marked unanswered instead of completed with plausible fiction?

Remove or rewrite anything that fails.
