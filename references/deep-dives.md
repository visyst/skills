# Optional Expression Deep Dives

Use these only after the core interview. Recommend the smallest set that closes a real gap. Ask which modules the founder wants before proceeding.

Each selected module should take one or two rounds. Continue using concrete situations and differentiated choices.

## Voice and language

### Use when

- the founder needs writing guidance;
- the personality is clear but its verbal expression is not;
- several audiences require a consistent parent voice;
- the current language sounds generic or unlike the founder.

### Explore

1. **Hidden-author test:** “Someone reads five sentences with the logo removed. What should make them suspect they came from this company?”
2. **Pressure test:** Ask how the company should sound when explaining a limitation, correcting a mistake, requesting payment, or disagreeing with a customer.
3. **Distance:** Explore expert versus peer, concise versus explanatory, restrained versus expressive, and serious versus lightly witty.
4. **Forbidden performance:** Identify language that would make the founder cringe or distrust the company.

### Produce

- defining voice quality;
- supporting qualities and boundaries;
- writing principles;
- preferred and avoided language;
- examples for ordinary, celebratory, and difficult moments.

## Messaging hierarchy

### Use when

- the company has several products, services, or audiences;
- the current revenue source differs from the desired future identity;
- the website risks becoming a catalogue of capabilities;
- the founder cannot explain the company briefly.

### Explore

1. **Five-second understanding:** What must a visitor understand before anything else?
2. **Proof order:** Which evidence should follow the main claim?
3. **Present versus future:** What is commercially true today, and what direction should lead the story?
4. **Channel roles:** What should the parent website, product pages, social presence, founder channels, support, and offline conversations each accomplish?

### Produce

- ordered key messages;
- short and expanded company descriptions;
- message roles by platform;
- claims that require evidence;
- messages that should remain quiet or contextual.

## Tagline exploration

### Use when

- the founder explicitly wants a tagline;
- the central brand idea is stable;
- the tagline has a clear job and placement.

### First establish the job

Ask whether the line must:

- explain what the company does;
- express a belief or attitude;
- unite several products;
- aid recall;
- accompany the logo;
- lead a temporary campaign.

Do not imply one line can perform every job.

### Explore territories

Derive 3–5 territories from the interview evidence. For each territory, explain:

- the idea it emphasizes;
- what it leaves out;
- where it could be used;
- the risk of sounding generic or overpromising.

Generate lines only after the founder selects territories. Separate:

- internal brand idea;
- company descriptor;
- permanent tagline candidate;
- campaign line.

Never present an untested line as timeless.

## Customer journey

### Use when

- the final document must guide product, service, retail, or support behavior;
- trust and experience are central differentiators;
- the audience has clear barriers or anxieties.

### Explore stages

Tailor the stages to the business:

1. discovery;
2. evaluation;
3. purchase or commitment;
4. onboarding or first use;
5. ordinary use or delivery;
6. support and failure recovery;
7. renewal, recommendation, or departure.

For each material stage, ask:

- What is the person worried about?
- What should they understand or feel next?
- What behavior would prove the brand?
- What would break trust?

### Produce

For each stage:

- desired perception;
- key message or action;
- evidence or trust signal;
- avoidable failure.

## Creative direction

### Use when

- a designer needs strategic direction but visual design is not being created;
- the founder can describe atmospheres more easily than aesthetics;
- the existing identity is fixed but the wider system is open.

### Explore

1. **World or place:** Offer atmospheres such as workshop, observatory, playground, independent publication, library, field station, gallery, neighborhood institution, or operating room—adapted to the brand.
2. **Sensory tension:** Ask which balance should be felt: movement/stability, precision/warmth, restraint/expression, digital/tactile, familiar/unexpected.
3. **Wrong costume:** Identify visual categories that would falsely position the company.
4. **Fixed inputs:** Confirm which marks, colors, typefaces, imagery, or cultural references must remain untouched.

### Produce

- desired atmosphere;
- interpretation and anti-interpretation;
- emotional balances;
- fixed inputs;
- signals the identity must avoid;
- evaluation questions for a designer.

Do not prescribe a palette, typeface, logo, or imagery style unless the founder separately requests design work.

## Re-entry checkpoint

After completing selected modules, summarize only what changed or became clearer. Then proceed to the final synthesis checkpoint in `SKILL.md`.
